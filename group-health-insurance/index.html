What is group health insurance for employees?

A group health insurance policy is a single master policy that a company buys to cover its employees against medical and hospital costs. Many employers extend it to spouses, children and parents as well. The company manages one policy for everyone, instead of each person buying their own.

Buyer Questions

Frequently asked questions about group health insurance.

Everything you need to know about group health coverage, onboarding, cashless claims, and policy renewal.

What is group health insurance?

A single policy bought by a company to cover its employees, and often their families, against medical and hospital costs.

Who can buy it?

Any registered business or organisation that wants to cover its employees. Eligibility depends on the insurer.

How many employees do we need?

Insurers set their own minimum group size. Talk to us and we will tell you what applies to your team.

Is it mandatory?

Not generally for private employers, though many offer it. Some statutory schemes may apply depending on company size and wages, so check with your advisor.

What affects the premium?

Team size, age mix, sum insured, who is covered, maternity and add-ons, and claims history.

How is it different from individual cover?

One company-managed policy with typically fewer waiting periods and less paperwork, but cover is linked to employment.

Are parents and pre-existing conditions covered?

Often yes, depending on the plan. Parents are usually an optional add-on, and terms vary by insurer.

What documents do we need?

Basic company details and an employee list. We will confirm the exact requirements when you request a quote.

How do claims and cashless treatment work?

Employees show their health card at a network hospital, and the claim is settled directly. Reimbursement claims are supported as well.

How does renewal work?

The policy renews each year. We review claims, team changes and needs with you, and adjust the plan.
